# B1 — The Glacier cost gotcha

What's the actual cost impact of an early deletion after archival? How would you redesign the
lifecycle so this stops quietly costing more than budgeted?


# B1 — Glacier Minimum Storage Duration and Deletion SLA

## Scenario

The current Terraform lifecycle rule transitions listing photos to S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval (`GLACIER`) 30 days after upload. A seller requests deletion on day 40, and the application deletes the photo immediately.

## Cost impact

S3 Glacier Flexible Retrieval has a 90-day minimum storage duration. The object has been in Glacier for only 10 days when it is deleted, leaving 80 days of the minimum duration unfulfilled.

AWS applies a prorated early-deletion storage charge for the remaining minimum duration. The actual amount depends on the object's billable size and the applicable regional storage rate. Transition-request and archived-object metadata charges may also apply.

Therefore, immediate deletion can satisfy the seller's deletion request while still incurring an additional storage charge.

## Recommended redesign

1. **Align storage class with the deletion requirement.** Keep photos that remain subject to user deletion in S3 Standard or evaluate S3 Intelligent-Tiering, whose applicable costs and access tiers should be assessed against the workload.
2. **Make archival eligibility explicit.** Use application-controlled metadata or object tags to identify photos that are genuinely eligible for long-term archival. Apply lifecycle transitions only to eligible objects, rather than assuming every photo can safely be archived after 30 days.
3. **Keep deletion independent of lifecycle transitions.** Record deletion requests, prevent further access as required, and ensure the application workflow deletes the relevant objects within the 30-day SLA.
4. **Treat existing Glacier objects separately.** If a deletion request arrives before the 90-day minimum is met, meet the deletion deadline and account for the applicable early-deletion charge rather than delaying deletion to avoid the fee.
5. **Validate the economics.** Compare storage savings with transition requests, metadata overhead, retrieval needs, and expected early-deletion charges before selecting a lifecycle policy.

## Trade-off

A fixed transition after 30 days does not account for when a seller may request deletion. Simply moving the transition to a later fixed day reduces some exposure but does not eliminate the risk if deletion requests can arrive at any time.

The policy must reflect actual retention requirements and deletion behavior. If photos remain eligible for deletion indefinitely, a storage class without the same minimum-duration exposure may be more appropriate than Glacier Flexible Retrieval.

## Conclusion

The correct design prioritizes the deletion SLA while controlling storage costs. Lifecycle policies should reflect business retention rules, and application-level deletion handling must remain effective regardless of the object's storage class.
